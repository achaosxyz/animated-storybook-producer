# Direction, exposure and cutting

Design what changes in the viewer's understanding before designing what moves. One primary focus per beat; the partner supports it and environment remains subordinate. Quiet holds are performance. Depth position and dramatic importance are separate axes.

## Paragraph and cut audit

Start with a composition that can hold both the essential action and its response. For every cut record:
1. Why leave this image now?
2. What new information or emotional distance does the next image supply?
3. What directs the eye across the cut: gaze, movement, object, shape or landmark?
4. When and why will the next image end?

Test whether a same-view change can express the beat, but do not impose a cut-count ceiling. A close-up is useful when a crucial detail, discovery or subjective feeling cannot read in the current view. Changing speaker is not sufficient reason. Audit the complete real camera sequence for redundant wide/detail/wide oscillation, not just individually defensible cuts. Baked-in framing changes count even when all runtime scales equal one.

Leave enough time to find, recognize and understand a result before returning to a partner. Sometimes a response can begin offscreen while the result remains visible. Sound may lead or trail a cut when its source and causal order stay clear. Preserve whole spoken boundaries and remap captions with the edit.

## Complete-pose acting

Use anticipation/contact/result/recovery only as needed to make the action readable. Each image is a complete pose, not a replacement arm. Match root/support landmarks and identity across exposure changes; do not normalize each alpha box to an identical size. Necessary two-person contact can use one complete contact-group sprite with both roots registered.

Pose replacement is not a camera cut. Held poses and purposeful time omission are honest limited animation; do not claim ungenerated continuous motion. Avoid pose crossfades, body stretching, fake lip-sync and continuous arm rotation. Blinks or local details require approved complete states or a genuinely safe independent element, not improvised anatomy.

## Motion language

Choose a few compatible behaviors per emotional passage: a deliberate camera reveal, a restrained whole-pose translation, a brief settling action, an environmental accent. Acceleration/deceleration should reflect attention and physical weight. Use overshoot only with an explicit expressive purpose, not as a default child-friendly preset. Stagger cause then response, not every layer by a fixed UI delay.

Establish space, direct attention, reveal, emphasize or follow: name the purpose of every camera move. Never substitute a zoom for a missing contact/action. A camera may transform real layers but cannot invent a new view from a flat image. Objects sharing contact need one coherent space; arbitrary parallax multipliers break grounding.

## Composition and transitions

Protect faces, identity marks, essential connections, grasp/support points, visible artwork edges and state-change regions. Decorative foregrounds may be intentionally cropped; they may not casually obscure the story. A planned close-up may omit nonessential body parts but must document intent and retain this beat's causal evidence. Never reclassify an accidental crop after seeing it fail.

A clean hard cut is often the correct choice. Dissolves, page turns and fades need a temporal, spatial or emotional meaning; do not use them to hide drifting props, broken anatomy or mismatched states. Shared-view exposures inherit camera continuity rather than briefly zooming in then snapping back.

Before bulk assets, play an internal animatic for expectation → change → result → response. Review cut-adjacent frames and contextual clips. Static checks, automated playback and realtime editorial perception are distinct evidence.
