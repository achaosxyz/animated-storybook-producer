# Story and adaptation

Use concrete experiences children recognize: waiting, losing an object, a changed plan, an unsuccessful attempt, wanting space or making room for a friend. Let both partners want, misjudge, act and repair. Avoid a permanent teacher/problem-child split, shame, threats and reward-for-obedience plots.

A useful arc is ordinary desire → friction → emotion noticed → attempt/choice → response and repair → a small resonant ending. This is a writing aid, not six mandatory titled beats. Use short natural speech and visible choices. Do not finish with a lecture addressed to “all good children.”

For Chinese families, use natural simplified Chinese rather than indiscriminate baby talk. Ground settings in everyday life rather than decorative cultural symbols. Put caregiver prompts outside the story; one open question is usually enough. Fiction is not clinical advice. Research factual developmental/health claims when they are actually needed; do not force web research for invented plot details. Show appropriate care around water, traffic, tools and food.

## From picture book to animation

First make the story coherent as a picture book. Then write emotional paragraphs with entry/exit relationship state, necessary visible cause, optional repetitive process and sound-carried information. A page turn, sentence, tool change or image state does not automatically create a shot.

Omit repetitive handling only when the actor, choice, essential contact and order of result remain intelligible. If a craft operation consumes the emotional story, redesign the operation; do not add explanatory close-ups to every step. An approved change of meaning or core causality requires renewed approval of that scope.

Prefer a clear adult storyteller with selective character dialogue. Narration introduces context and inner uncertainty; it should not repeat everything already visible. A warm opening can establish the shared activity before dialogue. Ratios such as 75% narration are optional drafting heuristics, never production quotas. Measure actual voiced time after synthesis; do not promise duration from character count.

## Gate A package

Provide title, logline, audience, complete narration/dialogue, emotional change, visual-only facts, and optional caregiver note. Identify practical asset/voice constraints without letting available files dictate the story. Check the ending resolves the relationship rather than merely finishing a task. Record the exact confirmed version and evidence; no confirmation means no production expansion.
